Numbers, Dates and Money in Hungarian Text

A small style sheet for anyone who writes or translates for Hungarian readers. English habits sneak in easily here: a stray decimal point, a date in the wrong order, a currency sign in front of the number. Each one is tiny, and together they make a text feel translated.

Dates: biggest first

Hungarian dates run from year to day, and the year gets a full stop after it.

English habitHungarian style
27 October 20262026. október 27.
27/10/20262026. 10. 27.
October 20262026 októberében

Month names are written in lower case. When a suffix follows a date written in figures, it is joined with a hyphen, for example október 27-én.

Times: the 24-hour clock

Times are written in 24-hour form, with a full stop or a colon between hours and minutes: 18.30 or 18:30. Pick one and stay with it across the whole text.

Decimals and thousands

Hungarian uses a decimal comma, not a point: 2,5 rather than 2.5. Numbers of five digits or more are grouped in threes with a space: 12 500 and 1 250 000. Four-digit numbers usually stay together: 5000. A comma inside a big number is the most common slip in translated copy, because readers will read it as a decimal.

Money

The currency comes after the amount, separated by a space: 5000 Ft, 20 € or 20 euró. Write the symbol or the word, not both, and keep the same choice throughout.

Percentages

A space is not needed before the percent sign in everyday Hungarian copy: 25%. With a suffix, the hyphen comes back: 25%-os.

A quick final check

  • Search the text for full stops between digits and decide whether each one should be a comma.
  • Read every date aloud. If it starts with the day, turn it round.
  • Look at every amount and make sure the currency stands after the number.
